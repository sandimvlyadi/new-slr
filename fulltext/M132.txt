Abstract
Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) occurs mostly in the elderly and often causes physical disability
and limitation. Early detection and intervention are particularly important in reducing the
deterioration of knee disease. Currently, early detection of KOA is mainly evaluated by
a Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) Classification with five class. KL is an ordered classification
problem. The cross-entropy loss (CEL) and fixed penalty loss (FPL) are commonly used in KL
system. Both CEL and FPL do not take into account the ease of classification between samples,
which results in that the obtained model has deficiency. In this paper, a novel focal ordinal loss
(FOL) is proposed by combining FPL and focal loss (FL) for KL. In the training algorithm
based on FOL, the difficult or easy example is first identified according to the predicted
probability toward to the true label at each epoch. The sample with a high predicted probability
is considered as an easy sample. On the contrary, it is considered as a difficult sample. The
weights for easy samples in the loss are then adjusted down in the next epoch, which results in
that the training process can focus on difficult samples. The performance of FOL is validated
on an X-ray image dataset from the Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI) with several classical
CNN models, such as VGG, Resnet, Densenet and Googlenet. The experimental results show
that FOL achieves significant improvements in many performance measures, especially in
mean square error (MSE). In addition, the experimental results on the augmented dataset
and the Resnet with the convolutional block attention module (CBAM) also show similar
improvements from FOL. It indicates that FOL is effective and superior to CEL and FPL in
the numerous models and two data types (original and augmented) for KL grading.

1 Introduction

Knee Osteoarthritis (KOA) is a frequent degenerative disease of the knee joint in middle
and elderly people that seriously affects the quality of life [1]. With the aging of the world
population, KOA has become an important public health issue. According to the research of
the World Health Organization, the current prevalence of progressive KOA is 10-15% in the
people over 60 years old. It is predicted that by 2050, the people aged above 60 years with
KOA will exceed 20% of the total global population, of which, one-third of KOA patients
will be disabled and physical impaired [2]. With high morbidity and disability rates, KOA
poses a serious threat to public health and increases the socio-economic burden [3]. However,
there is still no effective treatment for KOA. Therefore, early diagnosis of KOA is crucial to
prevent its deterioration [4].
    Imaging is an important approach in diagnosing KOA, such as MRI and X-ray [5]. MRI
is not used for routine KOA diagnosis due to its expensive equipment and high examination
costs, especially in backward areas [6], while X-ray is an economical, convenient and rapid
imaging modality that is widely used in clinical practice, especially in the examination of the
skeletal system. In addition, X-ray has an advantage in the contrast of bone structures [7]. In
1957, Kellgren and Lawrence proposed the KL classification system for KOA [8], in which
knee osteoarthritis is classified into five grades according the severity. An example and the
corresponding criteria for each grade are shown in Fig. 1.
    In determining KL grades, the experience and carefulness of doctor plays a decisive role.
Different doctors often give different results on the same image because the criteria for KL
grading are relatively vague as well as different experiences. Statistically, the reliability of KL
graders currently is not high, which ranges from approximately 0.67 to 0.73 [9]. In addition,
misclassifying a grade of the knee as a nearby grade (e.g. grade 0 to grade 1) is far less serious
than misclassifying a grade as a distant grade (e.g. grade 0 to grade 4). Therefore, it is not
sufficient only using the accuracy of grading alone and the mean squared error (MSE) [10]
may be a better solution in evaluating the performance of grading KOA.
    There are many existing works in grading KOA. It is a regular way to combine extracted
features and classification method to address KL grading issues. Brahim et al. [11] used inde-
pendent component analysis for feature extraction, and then used random forest approach to
obtain a binary classification result of 82.98%. Subramoniam et al. [12] used local binary

with Euclidean, cosine, and martingale for KL grading and obtained a result of 93%. Fur-

location. Shamir et al. [13] used template matching to localize the knee joint position on X-

images, the edges of femur and tibia were then extracted by Canny operator. Their method
obtained a binary classification accuracy of 99.19%.
    In recent years, many convolutional neural network (CNN), such as Resnet [16], VGG [17],
Googlenet [18] and Densenet [19] have been widely used in various medical classification
tasks such as liver cancer detection and diabetic retinopathy detection [20–23]. In contrast,
the research with deep learning for KL classification of osteoarthritis is still in its start-up
phase [24]. Antony et al. [25, 26] designed a new CNN model with a weighted combination
of cross-entropy loss (CEL) and mean-squared error loss and obtained an accuracy of 63.6%.
Marc et al. [27] proposed a novel end-to-end CNN framework to automatically quantify
the severity of knee osteoarthritis and achieved a recognition result of 64.3%. Tiulpin et
al. [28] used a deep siamese network to score the severity grade of knee osteoarthritis and
achieved an accuracy of 66.7%. Unfortunately, the above approaches do not make full use of
the relationships between labels and the manifold of the data. To improve these drawbacks,
Nasser et al. [29] proposed an autoencoder network with discriminative information loss
and achieved an average accuracy of 82.53% in binary classification. After that, Nguyen et
al. [30] used a deep semi-supervised learning method based on manifold learning to train a
valid model and obtained an accuracy of 70.9%. In addition, some scholars investigated KL
classification from the perspective of object detection. Liu et al. [31] proposed a novel object
detection method for automatic scoring of KL classification by an improved faster R-CNN
and obtained a multi-classification accuracy of 71.1%. Norman et al. [32] used U-net to
extract the ROI of the knee and exploited the Densenet to classify KL grades, and achieved
an average sensitivity of 77.2% and a specificity of 91.5%.
    It is a common practice in machine learning to impose a larger penalty to difficult sample.
For example, the classical AdaBoost algorithm [33] gradually increased the penalty weight
of difficult samples through serial training. Lin et al. [34] proposed focal loss (FL) to focus
on the difficult samples in object detection and obtained an improved accuracy. Chen et al.
[35] assigned higher penalty for the predicted results that are further away from the actual
classes with a fixed penalty loss (FPL). It makes the predicted KL grades to converge more
easily to the actual grades. However, the drawback of this approach is that the penalty weight
matrix cannot be adaptively adjusted during the training process. The difficult samples often
change during training.
    Attention mechanisms [36] have shown great success in various computer vision tasks such
as image classification [37–41] and image segmentation [42–52]. A representative of attention
mechanism is the convolutional block attention module (CBAM) [53] where both channel
attentional and spatial attentional mechanism are introduced to extract features. Zhang et al.
[54] proposed a CBAM-CNN for knee osteoarthritis, in which a Resnet was applied to first
extract knee features from radiographs and then the knee features are combined with the
features extracted by the CBAM to automatically perform KL prediction. However, it does
not take into account the difficult samples.

    To address the shortcomings of existing works on automatic classification of KL classes
 for osteoarthritis of the knee, we propose a novel focal ordinal loss. The focal ordinal loss
 focuses the difficult samples in each epoch of training by reducing the weights of easy-to-
 classify samples at next epoch. In addition, CBAM is introduced into our model and some
 data-augmentation schemes are adopted. The experiments with some classical CNN network
 models show that the focal ordinal loss performs better than the cross-entropy loss as well
 as the fixed penalty weights loss. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
  (i) A novel focal ordinal loss is proposed by combining the focal loss and the fixed penalty
      loss.
 (ii) The focal ordinal loss obtains a better performance than the cross-entropy loss and the
      fixed penalty loss in the automatic classification of KL classes for osteoarthritis of the
      knee.
(iii) The focal ordinal loss also performs well in the model with data augmentation and CBAM.
    The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 briefly introduces the fix

 and CBAM are presented in Sect. 3. In Sect. 4, extensive numerical experiments are carried
 out and the corresponding results are discussed. Finally, the conclusion and future work are
 drawn in Sect. 5.

classification performance. Liu et al. [31] have added the FL to object detection in the knee
ROI, and obtained good localization results. The FL is a variant of the standard CEL. The

3 Materials and Methodology

In this section, the dataset used in this paper is first introduced. Then, a data prepossessing
scheme is carried out to mitigate the problem of class imbalance. Finally, a new focal ordinal
loss function is proposed to improve the deficiencies of CEL and FPL in the KL grade of
knee osteoarthritis.

3.1 Dataset

The images used to assess knee x-rays in this paper are taken from the Osteoarthritis Initiative
(OAI) [56], a study of knee osteoarthritis. There are 4796 participants between 45 to 79 years
old. The dataset collated from Chen et al. [35] contains 4130 X-ray images and all images
are randomly split into training, validation and test sets according to the ratio of 7: 1: 2. The
distribution of the dataset is shown in Fig. 5.

3.2 Data Preprocessing

From Fig. 5, we see that the distribution of samples is unbalanced. The proportion of samples
with grade 0 (normal condition) and grade 2 (mild symptoms) in the total sample are 39.38%
and 26.33% respectively, while the proportions of samples with grade 3 (moderate symptoms)
and grade 4 (severe symptoms) are only 13.15% and 0.03% respectively. To alleviate the
unbalanced problem in the size of classes, we augment the samples of minority classes.
   Considering that all the knee joint images in the original dataset are vertically placed and
that the knee joints are all located in the center of the images, we use cropping, contrast
enhancement and image filtering to augment the samples of minority classes.

                                                                                     123
5206                                                                                 W. Liu et al.




Fig. 5 The distribution of the dataset in this paper




    Cropping: As the main factors for knee osteoarthritis are the lower femur and the upper
tibia, all of these areas are preserved when cropping. For an image, we crop 15% from its
top, bottom, left and right directions respectively. The cropped image is 160 pixels in length
and width. In order to meet the requirements of the size of input image, the cropped image
is scaled up to 224 × 224 that is the size of the image before cropping.
    Contrast enhancement: Suppose AGV is the average grayscale value over all pixel points
for an image. If AGV ≤ 130, which means the overall grayscale values are dark, they are
scaled to the interval [0, 255], otherwise the grayscale values remain unchanged.
    Image filtering: The mean filtering with 7 × 7 template is used. Mean filtering makes the
image smoother and has a denoising effect.
    Data augmentation is performed only in the training and validation sets. Different data
augmentation schemes are designed for each classification so that the number of samples in
each classification is basically balanced.
    The size of grade 0 is the largest. Thus, not any data augmentation is performed in grade 0
and other categories will be augmented to this size. For grade 1, the samples are cropped so
that the number of augmented samples is twice as large as before. For grade 2, half of samples
are randomly selected for cropping so that the number of augmented samples reaches 1.5
times of the previous one. For grade 3, the original sample set is cropped and enhanced by
contrast respectively, so that the number of augmented samples is three times the original
one. For grade 4, cropping, contrast enhancement and mean filtering are all used so that the
number of augmented samples is 12 times of the previous one. The augmentation scheme is
shown in Figs. 6, 7 and the distribution of samples in the training and validation sets after
augmentation is shown in Fig. 8.
    The images in the dataset used in this paper are all 224 × 224 single-channel grayscale
images, rather than RGB images. In order to meet the input requirements of Resnet, the
single-channel grayscale images are simply copied to compose three-channel images. In
addition, the images are normalized by the z-score.

3.3 Focal Ordinal Loss Function

The FPL does not focus on the difficult samples. In this section, a new loss, the focal ordinal
loss (FOL), is proposed by combining the focal loss and the fixed penalty loss. The proposed
FOL not only has the characteristics of fixed penalty loss, but also can focus on the difficult
samples.
   Figure 9 is a schematic diagram about the FOL of a sample whose correct class is grade
1. From Fig. 9, FOL focuses on the difficult samples or incorrectly classified samples by
lowering the loss weight of the easy samples or correctly classified samples.

4.2 Experimental Design

In order to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, four classical CNN networks
are used for validation. The four classical CNN networks are VGG, Resnet, Densenet and
Googlenet. Experiments are conducted with the original data approach, the data augmentation
approach and the CBAM approach respectively, where the CBAM is only applied in the
Resnet. The setting of hyper-parameters has a great impact on the model performance. In
this paper, following the parameter setting of Chen et al [35], the initial learning rate is set
to 5 × 10−4 and a discrete staircase is used to reduce the learning rate by multiplying it with
0.8 every five training rounds. And the Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) is used for model
optimization. The penalty matrix of the fixed penalty loss and the initial penalty matrix of

Fig. 10 A comparison of confusion matrices among the three loss functions



    As can be seen from Tables 1 and 2, the proposed FOL outperforms the baseline methods
in most of the performance measurements on the original dataset and enhanced dataset
at all levels of the Resnet model, especially in the performance of MSE. On the original
dataset, MSE is decreased by 12.11% on average compared to the baseline models, while
it is decreased by 18.82% on the enhanced dataset. In addition, the proposed FOL achieves
substantial improvements in all the performance measurements except for ACC that is slightly
inferior to CEL and the FPL.
    To demonstrate the benefits of FOL more intuitively, confusion matrices for the three test
sets are used for comparison. As an example, Fig. 10 shows the compared results of ResNet-
50 on the augmented datasets. To ensure fairness in the comparison, the chosen three test
sets have similar ACC.
    In Fig. 10, the rows of each confusion matrix represent the true labels of the samples
and the columns represent the predicted labels. Due to the ordinal nature of the KL grades,
a better model should classify the samples as correctly as possible and minimize the gap
between the predicted labels and the true labels to the misclassified samples. As shown in
the red dashed box in Fig. 10, both the number of samples misclassified to grade 0 for CEL
and FPL are greater than the ones misclassified to grade 1 while the true label is grade 2. In
contrast, the FOL achieves an effective improvement in the gap. This indicates that the FOL
can effectively guide the model to cluster the predicted labels towards the true labels. This
is the main reason that the MSE of FOL is far better than the two baseline losses although
ACC is not much different.
    In order to verify the generality of the FOL, we also carry out the comparison experi-
ments with Densenet, VGG and Googlenet networks. Table 3-6 list the comparison results,
respectively.
    The results in Tables 3, 4, 5, 6 are similar to those of Resnet. The proposed FOL achieves
the significant improvements in all measures except for ACC. It verifies the effectiveness and
the type of the FOL compared to CEL and FPL.

    As can be seen from Tables 7, 8, the models with FOL achieve the best performances except
for ACC. On the original datasets, CBAM-Resnet with FOL showed an average improvement
of 0.51% in ACC, 20.46% in MSE and 3.49% in KAPPA compared to the baseline models. On
the augmented datasets, the improvements in ACC, MSE and KAPPA are 0.91%, 19.23%
and 3.59% respectively. The models with FOL perform very well in MSE. It provides an
improvement of 20.46%. In terms of ACC, it is slightly inferior to that with CEL. However,
it is still better than the ones with FPL.
    To visualize the difference between the models with CBAM and without CBAM, the heat
map of a sample with Grade 0 from the validation set in Fig. 11 is shown. A heat map can
reflect the “importance parts” of an image. The darker the colour of a part, the more the
network tends to base its final prediction on that part of the image.
    As can be seen in Fig. 11, the model without CBAM tends to make KL grade predictions
with the intact interosseous gaps, while the model with CBAM focuses on the left and right
interosseous gaps and ignores the right-central interosseous gaps. Thus, the model with
CBAM identifies the hallmark features of knee osteoarthritis (e.g. narrow interosseous gaps,
osteophytes, etc.) more easily. In addition, the model of FOL also performs better than that
of CE.
    In summary, FOL provides a stable improvement in most of performance measures.


5 Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we propose FOL to address the shortcomings of CEL and FPL in KL grading
task for knee osteoarthritis. FOL can focus on the difficult samples by adjusting down the loss

5222                                                                                               W. Liu et al.


weight of the samples with high prediction probability toward to the true label at each epoch
of training. The performance of FOL is validated on the X-ray image data from OAI with
several classical CNN models such as VGG, Resnet and Densenet. The experimental results
show that FOL obtains significant improvement in most of performance measures, especially
in MSE. In addition, FOL also shows similar improvement on the augmented dataset and
CBAM-Resnet compared with CEL and FPL. In a word, FOL performs better than CEL and
FPL in KL grading task. The results can provide a valuable reference to policymakers in
decision making.
   There are some limitations in our work. Some improvement directions are as follows.
1. The improvement of FOL in ACC is not stable. We will investigate it and improve this
   shortcoming.
2. The hyper-parameters in this paper are refer to the default value of Chen et al. [35]. They
   are certainly not optimal. We will tune them in the following work.
3. Different weights lead to different results on FOL. In this paper, we refer to the weight
   matrix setting of Chen et al. [35] as our setting. The optimal weight matrix is not tuned.
   We will consider it as a future work.
4. Referring to Lin et al. [34], the gamma value of focal loss is set to 2. It is not optimal on
   all indicators. We will consider to tune the optimal gamma in the future work.
