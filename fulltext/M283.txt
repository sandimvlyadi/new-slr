       Abstract. Osteoarthritis (OA) of the knee is the most prevalent joint disease that
       may cause disability. The Kellgren-Lawrence rating system is widely used to
       classify the severity of OA and is often used to diagnose it by interpreting X-ray
       imaging. The goal of this research was to create a model that uses deep learning that
       could automatically identify the degree of severity of knee OA, since diagnosing
       the condition required expertise and time. First, we examined the performance of
       10 cutting-edge deep learning models. Individual state-of-the-art models were able
       to obtain the maximum accuracy of 0.69. Second, to solve the class imbalance,
       we adjusted the training technique, which increased the accuracy to 0.70. Lastly,
       shallow neural networks and majority voting were used in ensemble models. Our
       ensemble model Knee X-Net produced the best accuracy, delivering a precision
       of 0.72 that compares well with the most advanced techniques.

       Keywords: — Knee osteoarthritis · Kellgren-Lawrence grading system ·
       ensemble learning


1 Introduction
Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disorder. It is one of the most common causes
leading to disability especially in the elderly [1]. Moreover, the knee joint is the most
common joint that is affected by OA [2], with the global prevalence of knee OA being
22.9% in people aged 40 years old and over [3]. There are also other factors apart
from age that contribute to knee OA, including gender, genetic factors, obesity, injury,
inactivity, and daily activities [1, 2, 4, 5]. Individuals with osteoarthritis (OA) may have
knee discomfort, stiffness of the joints joint swelling, and challenges doing regular tasks
[4, 5]. In the worst case, knee OA can lead to disability [6]. There are many imaging
techniques that can be used to diagnose knee OA, but X-ray is the most common method
owing to its low cost and wide accessibility [6]. The features of the knee OA disease
are joint space narrowing, osteophyte formation, cyst formation, subchondral sclerosis,
and coronal tibiofemoral subluxation [5]. The severity is usually classified into 5 grades,
ac- cording to Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grading system. This includes None (Grade

    One of the most prevalent orthopedic disorders brought on by ageing is osteoarthritis
(OA), which causes structural alterations in the afflicted joints as well as inflammation
of the bursa ligaments, synovial membranes, and cartilage [7]. It strains the healthcare
system in addition to causing a significant health burden [8]. Over time, OA may lead
to impairment since its prevalence and incidence rise annually in the population. The
number of people with osteoarthritis in the hip and knee (OAK) grew to almost 300 mil-
lion worldwide in 2019 [9]. Hip and knee OA is ranked eleventh globally among causes
contributing to disability. Years spent with disabilities (YLDs) [10] are the main metric
used to quantify this incapacitating impact. Given that the knee is the biggest joint in the
human body, it is significant to highlight that knee OA is among the most prevalent kinds
of OA [11–13]. Furthermore, in advanced stages of knee OA, joint replacements may
become necessary [14–16]. As China’s population ages, osteoarthritis (OA) is expected
to rank among the primary causes of impairment and social expenses for the elderly
[17]. The Global Cost of Disease (GBD) Report 2019 reports that China’s incidence
of osteoarthritis (OA) increased by 128.7% from 1990 to 2019 to 84.258/100,000 [18].
Concerns with knee OA are growing [19]. Researchers discovered that weight, age, and
gender are separate risk factors for knee OA. Among these, obesity raised the likelihood
of OA by three times [20–22], and knee OA is highly correlated with an increase in BMI
[23]. A review of the literature [24] also revealed a 35% greater incidence of knee OA
was linked to a 5-unit rise in body BMI. Obesity and overweight have become major
public health issues on a global scale. There were 603.7 million obese people in 2015
[25]. When a person’s body mass index (BMI) falls between 25 and 29.9 kg/m2, they
are considered overweight; when their BMI is more than 30, they are considered obese.
A BMI of ≥25 kg/m2 is considered high [26]. YLDs and life years adjusted for dis-
ability are the main metrics used to assess the effects of knee OA (DALYs). High BMI
is the reason that sticks out the most among the 15 most prevalent causes of DALYs
worldwide from 2010 to 2019, and it has the largest change rate [27]. Between 1990
and 2017, there was a notable global rise in the YLDs linked to high BMI [27]. China’s
obesity rate rose from 60th place in the world in 1975 to second place in 2014 [22].
Numerous studies [15, 29–31] investigated the relationship between high BMI and knee
OA burden. Nevertheless, there isn’t much research comparing the impact of knee OA
in China with other countries or regions, nor are there many studies analysing the cost
of knee OA brought on by high BMI in China. Moreover, there is no evidence of its
correlation with the Chinese sociodemographic index, age, or gender. To get around

ensemble methods demonstrated their potential for use in OA grading, improving the
overall accuracy to 0.72, which compares favorably with the baseline models.

2 Method
2.1 Dataset
We employed the Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI) dataset [33] in this study, which con-
tained 5 classes according to the KL grading system. The data was split into train,
validation, and test sets with a ratio of 7:1:2, resulting in 5778, 826, and 1656 images
in the three sets respectively. The models developed in this study were evaluated on
the same unseen test set. The size of the images was of 224 × 224. The distribution of
samples in each class for train, validation, and test sets is shown in Fig. 1. One can see
that the dataset exhibits severe class imbalance, which is a common problem in medical
image analysis.

2.2 Baseline Models
This project compared the performance among 10 models including GoogLeNet,
ResNet-34, ResNet-50 [34], VGG-16, VGG-19 [35], Mobile Net [36], DenseNet-121,
DenseNet-161 [37], Efficient Net [38], and ResNet-18, [39]. All models were pretrained
with ImageNet dataset before being trained with the knee OA dataset. The models were
trained with 30 epochs and a batch size of 28. The optimizer was Adam with an ini-
tial learning rate of 0.0001, and it was reduced by a factor of 10 every 5 epochs. In
addition, augmentation techniques were used to increase the variety of training images.
This included random horizontal flipping with a probability of 0.5, adjusting brightness
factor from 0.5 and 1.2, adjusting saturation factor between 0.5 and 1.5, rotation within
5 degrees, and random translation within a range of 0.1 of the image size.
     Two experiments were carried out to study the model performance for OA grading.
Experiment 1 compared the performance of 10 state-of-the-art models, trained with
cross entropy loss. In Experiment 2, to address class imbalance problem in this data
set, a weighted sampling strategy was added during training the baseline models. This
sampling method assigned probability to each class, which was inversely proportional
to the sample size of each training class. Each model was trained and tested for 3 times.
Precision, recall, F1 score, and test accuracy were recorded.

2.3 Ensemble Models
All 10 models in each experiment were combined to form an ensemble model. We used
two strategies: majority voting and shallow neural network.
    In majority voting, we passed the inputs to each model, and each model output 5
logits corresponding to class 0 to class 4. These logits were then passed through a softmax
function which output probabilities of each class. The probabilities of the same class in
10 models were added, and the ensemble model returned the class with the highest value
as the final prediction.
    In the shallow neural network method, we designed a 2- layer fully connected neural
network (FCN) for ensembling. We first input the image to the 10 trained models and
con- catenated the logits from each model into a 50-dimensional vector, which was fed
into the FCN for prediction. The FCN was trained for 30 epochs using cross entropy
loss, with the same training parameters as baseline models. The training and testing were
repeated 3 times.

3.5 Explainability of the Model
Finally, to assess the usefulness of the prediction, Smooth- Grad CAM++ [15] was
applied to the best-performing model to visualize the region of the image that influenced
the model to make a prediction. DenseNet-161 trained with the weighted sampling
strategy, which was a single model with the highest accuracy, was selected to study
the explain- ability of the model. Figure 5 showed that the model focused on the field
between the bones (joint space narrowing), which is one of the areas used to classify
OA severity [5].


4 Discussion and Conclusion
The performance of ten cutting-edge models for classifying knee osteoarthritis and
automating the evaluation of X-ray images using the Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) evalu-
ation method was examined, and the findings were published in this publication. The
highest accuracy of 0.69 was achieved by ResNet-50, VGG-16, VGG-19, DenseNet-
161, ResNet-34, DenseNet-121, with the last two being the most robust models due to
their small standard deviation (0.01). However, the individual model was not effective
in classifying images of class 1 potentially due to the class imbalance problem. Hence,
a different sampling method, the weighted sampling strategy was investigated during
model training. This strategy was shown to improve the F1 score of class 1; however, the
overall accuracy did not in- crease. The best model trained with this sampling strategy
was DenseNet-161, achieving an accuracy of 0.70 ± 0.01.
    Moreover, ensemble techniques including majority voting and shallow neural net-
work were used to improve the overall model performance. The ensemble model, fusing
the models trained without the weighted sampling strategy and trained with a shallow
neural network yielded the highest accuracy (0.72 ± 0.01). This result was higher than
another work using an ensemble model of three DenseNet-121 trained with different
random seeds, which achieved an average multiple-class accuracy of 0.71 [40].
    We believe this project can help clinicians to diagnose knee OA easily and effi-
ciently. In addition, it is expected to be particularly useful in hospitals where there are
no specialists in reading knee radiographs.
