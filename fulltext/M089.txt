Abstract
Osteoarthritis (OA) is a common form of knee arthritis which causes significant disability
and is threatening to plague patient’s quality of life. Although this chronic condition does
not lead to fatality, still there exists no known cure for OA. Diagnosis of OA can be
confirmed primarily based on radiographic findings. Being a progressive disease, early
identification of OA is crucial for clinical interventions to curtail the OA degeneration.
Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grading system has been traditionally employed to assess the
knee OA severity. Due to the recent advancements of deep learning in computer vision,
more studies have employed deep neural network in automatically predicting KL grade
from plain knee joint radiograph. However, these studies treat KL grading as a multi-class
classification task and ignore the inherent ordinal nature within the KL grades. In this
study, we propose an ordinal regression module for neural networks to treat KL grading
as an ordinal regression task. Our module takes an input from neural network and
produces 4 cut-points to partition the prediction space into 5 respective KL grades. The
proposed model is optimized by a cumulative-link loss function. Performance of the
model is evaluated against various notable neural networks and significant improvements
on the knee OA KL grade prediction were demonstrated.

Keywords Cumulative-link loss function . Kellgren-Lawrence grade . Knee radiograph . Ordinal
regression module . Osteoarthritis

1 Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most common form of arthritis, causing significant disability for patients
worldwide [21]. Although OA does not result in death, it greatly impacts mobility, and quality of
life [1, 9, 17]. This degenerative joint disease occurs frequently in hands, hips, and knees where
the cartilage tissues within the joint begin to break down and the underlying bone begins to
deform [19]. These changes develop slowly; worsen over time while causing physical discomfort.
OA is lacking effective clinical treatments across different stages. Patients with end-stage knee
OA are often left with total knee replacement (TKR) surgery [34], which is highly invasive and
costly. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, about 32.5 million of United
States adults are diagnosed with OA [1]. The annual rate of TKR surgery for individual age 45 to
64 has doubled since the year 2000. Early diagnosis on OA is crucial for clinical interventions in
curtailing progression and mitigating future disability [25, 29].
    Despite the introduction of several imaging modalities such as MRI [4, 7, 18], OCT [7, 11],
and ultrasound, radiograph continue to be the most widely applied in clinical setting to diagnose
knee OA given that it is safe, cost-efficiency and wide accessibility [8]. In 1957, Kellgren-
Lawrence (KL) grading system [16] was proposed for epidemiological studies on OA. KL is a
semi-quantitative grading system which split the OA severity into 5 progression levels where
grade 0 indicated healthy knee joint while grade 4 indicates severe knee OA. In 1961, this
grading system was accepted by World Health Organization (WHO) as radiological definition
for OA epidemiological studies [27, 28] and became the common grading standard for OA
severity classification in clinical diagnosis. However, this grading system is heavily relied on
the physician’s experience and carefulness while subject to intra-observer and inter-observer
variability where the former ranges from 0.67 to 0.73 [6]. The low reliability of physician’s
grading is believed due to cause misclassification of the KL grade to its nearby grade [5].
    High prevalence of knee OA prompts an urgent need for accurate, reproducible, and reliable
knee OA severity classification algorithm. Over the past decades, deep learning methods
achieve state-of-the-art performances in various computer vision tasks such as cell detection
and segmentation in histopathological images [12, 31], blood vessel segmentation [15], white
matter lesion segmentation from brain MRI [20], breast cancer analysis on histology image
[24], paediatric bone age assessment [14], knee joint localization from MRI [36], and so on.
    There are several studies apply deep learning based method on plain radiograph knee OA
analysis. Antony et al. (2016) proposed to use convolution neural network to quantify the
severity of knee OA [2] and this project was further expand to optimize the network using
weighted ratio of categorical cross-entropy and mean squared loss functions [3]. Tiulpin et al.
(2019) presented a modified Siamese network to measure the KL score of plain radiographs
[33]. These studies treating KL grades as nominal variables which do not have an intrinsic
order. Recently, Chen et al. (2019) created a novel ordinal loss function to train deep neural
networks in grading the severity of knee OA [5]. Ordinal variable is like nominal variable, but
the former takes ordering into account. Although KL grading classification can be treated as
nominal variables and executed with multi-class classification, however, we should appreciate
and consider the ordinality essence of the KL grading as the progressive grading provides extra
information. Chen et al. (2019) showcase that by emphasizing the ordinality and ranking
information for model training can reduce the faraway grade misclassification such as
misclassified a grade 4 OA as grade 1 and vice versa. This study inspires us to approach
KL severity prediction as an ordinal regression instead of conventional multi-class

classification task. A correct prediction of KL score on radiograph helps to discover early stage
of OA (KL score 1 and 2) and allowed early clinical interventions.
   The contributions of our work are as follows. First, we proposed an ordinal regression module
(ORM) to the classification neural networks to perform ordinal regression. With the proposed
module, ordinality of the KL grading can be preserved and considered. We also optimize the neural
networks using cumulative link (CL) loss function. Second, we performed a comparison study
against 23 neural networks from 6 notable classification neural network architectures.
   This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 explains the data acquisition and processing.
Section 3 illustrates the methodology of our experiment in detail. Section 4 reports the
comparative performances of classification neural network architectures between multi-class
classification and ordinal regression classification methods. Section 5 discuss about the
findings while Section 6 addresses the conclusion and future work.


2 Data acquisition and processing

We acquired data from Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI), a large-scale natural history OA database
which contains clinical evaluation data, biospecimen repository, and multi-modality biomedical
images. OAI is publicly accessible (https://nda.nih.gov/oai/) to enable the worldwide scientific
community to better understand progression of OA and develop new treatments to slow
progression or modify this disabling disease. The main reason in choosing OAI is because OAI is
the current largest OA database, featuring 4796 participants. As our study focuses on KL gradings,
out of the multi-sequences, we chose knee bilateral posterior-anterior fixed flexion radiographs from
the baseline cohort to evaluate our proposed methodology.
    These raw radiographs come in different resolutions therefore require preprocessing to
standardize the images. A series of preprocessing was performed onto the radiographs such as
resize the physical resolution of all radiographs to 0.14 mm/pixel and cropped the central
region of the resized image. The cropped images shall contain the whole knee joint structure
where femur and tibia are clearly visible as shown in Fig. 1. Each of the cropped knee joint
images is pre-labeled with a KL grade, provided by OAI. A total of 4130 pairs of knee joints
annotated with KL grades were harvested from the baseline cohort. These knee joints were
further stratified into train, valid, and test datasets in a ratio of 7:1:2 with balance distribution
across each KL grade. Test images were isolated from the model training process to avoid data
leakage and to ultimately access the model’s performances.
    In general, OAI obtained the KL gradings by having two expert readers independently
accessed each radiograph, blinded to each other’s reading. Pre-specified discrepancies between
readers were adjudicated in a consensus session with blinded viewed simultaneously and a
third reader participating. If the score was not adjudicated for discrepancies, the scoring given
by the senior reader, an experienced musculoskeletal radiologist will be the final.

predicting task of knee radiographs. Several state-of-the-art architectures such as VGG [30],

3.2 Predictive techniques

3.2.1 Baseline: Multi-class KL grade prediction

Neural networks mentioned in Section 3.1 were all pre-trained with ImageNet and produced
1000 outputs. To utilize the pre-trained weights and biases, we replaced the last FC layer. The
new FC layer retains the same input size while producing 5 outputs which corresponding to the
5 possible KL grades. The output nodes were passed into a softmax layer to generate the
probability for each of the nodes. The node with highest probability represents the prediction
class. Cross entropy loss was used as the optimization function. Results obtained from this
method become the baseline for our comparison.

3.2.2 Proposed methodology: Ordinal regression KL grade prediction

Classifying a radiograph involves predicting an image into several classes which have a natural
severity ordering, i.e. KL grades 0 to 4. Conventional multi-class classification task considers
each grade as mutually exclusive output, ignoring the order/severity ranking. Therefore, we
propose ORM, a module that can be integrated with conventional neural network to perform
classification on ordered classes. Unlike softmax layer in traditional classification model that
predicts the instance as 1 given the largest probability values, our ORM partitions the
probability space of the scalar output into respective K classes with K − 1 cut-points. As refer
to Fig. 1, new FC layer is having a single output node which is channeled into ORM to
produce 5 output nodes corresponding to the KL grades.

4 Results

As mentioned in Section 3.1, we involved 6 different classification neural network architec-
tures, i.e., VGG, GoogLeNet, ResNet, DenseNet, ResNeXt, and Mobilenet v2, resulting in a
total of 21 variants of model to evaluate our proposed ORM (Section 3.2.2) against the
baseline (Section 3.2.1), and results reported by Chen et al. [5]. To the best of our knowledge,
Chen et al. (2019) is the only study that considering the ordinality of the KL grades by using
ordinal loss function. All these models were fed with the testing datasets (~1656 images) and
evaluation were measured in terms of MAE, ACCMacro, and QWK.

5 Discussion

Acknowledging the ordinal essence of KL grade for a classification neural network is
achievable and feasible through adding ORM into the architectures. By having ORM, neural
networks learn about the ordinal relationships between the KL grades. In general, our ORM
approach not only outperformed baseline approach, but also surpassed the results of Chen et al.
[5], by demonstrating lower MAE and higher values for ACCMacro and QWK. It is worth to
mention that the models of [5] were fine-tuned while our models (baseline and ORM) were
trained with default parameter settings.
    As mentioned earlier, the cut-points of the ORM were optimized through the model’s error
back-propagation process. As refer to Fig. 2, the distance between the cut-points are slowly
increase as the epoch running. By using the CL loss function that is specifically designed for
ordinal regression, the cut-points were slowly adjusted to minimize the model’s loss and
maximize the model’s performances. To prevent the orders of the cut-points from overrun, we
performed clamping mechanism to ensure each cut-point remained in their corresponding
prediction space. The clamping mechanism helps the model to obtain favorable cut-points
faster and easier.
    As refer to the contingency matrices in Fig. 3, ORM approach correctly identified the KL
grades (70% and above) for all grades except Grade 1 which only achieve 38.51%. Similarly,

   The knee joint radiographs of Fig. 4 are of KL grade 4 (most severe OA). From the two
misclassified images, we can observe that the bone-on-bone contact at the tibia femoral joint is
not as severe as the two correctly predicted images. Although the images are labeled with KL
grade 4, but their degree of joint space narrowing (JSN) are differed. This might cause the
model to misclassify the non-extreme cases as less severe OA (KL grade 3), which are only 1
grade away from the ground truth.
   According to the KL grading system, grade 0 is defined as healthy knee with definite
absence of radiographic features of OA while grade 1 joints are having doubtful JSN and
possible osteophytic lipping which also known as osteophytes. The knee joint radiographs in
Fig. 5 shows misclassification of low KL grades images. These misclassifications are un-
avoidable due to the radiographic features are not obvious and thus causes the classifications to
be ambiguous. However, we must acknowledge the ability of ORM in reducing the faraway
grade misclassification cases.
   Like most of the scientific experiments, several limitations exist in our study. First, all the
models were trained using standardized hyperparameters; a fine-tuned model could further
improve the classification performance. Not only that, we can also optimize this model for
early detections of OA by implementing weighted penalty to further reduce the false positive
and false negative predictions especially in KL grade 0 and 1. Second, our study focused on a
single cohort dataset: OAI baseline cohort dataset. Future work is to expand our experiments
into longitudinal cohort analysis. Besides, we utilized the pretrained ImageNet weights without
performing any transfer-learning mechanism. We allowed the weights and bias of all layers in
the neural network architecture to update their values according to the distributions of the data
points.


6 Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed to treat KL grading on plain knee joint radiographs as an ordinal
classification task instead of conventional multi-class classification. Our ORM was designed to
Multimedia Tools and Applications


classify ordinality categorical variables. Neural networks trained with our approach
outperformed those with baseline approach in terms of MAE, ACCMacro, and QWK.
DenseNet-161 is having the best performances with lowest MAE (0.330) and highest
ACCMacro (88.09%) and QWK (0.8609) among all architectures. Not only that, our ORM
approach also outperformed Chen’s study in QWK for all architectures. In addition, these
neural networks were trained with default parameter setting. We strongly believed that fine-
tuning these models will further reduce the MAE and increase the ACCMacro and QWK.
Indeed, this will become our future work. The proposed ORM along with the CL loss function
is having superior potential in ordinal classification tasks and is compatible with most of the
popular state-of-the-art classification neural network architectures. Ultimately, we aspire to
further improve and expand this approach to other ordinality classification task.
