1. Introduction

    Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative disorder the prevalence of which is exacerbated by global population ageing [1]. The
most common musculoskeletal disorder in the world, OA is also the most common type of arthritis that causes severe dis-
ability in its victims [2]. The main cause of frailty in older and overweight adults is OA [3–5]. The joint that is most frequently
impacted by OA is the knee, followed by the hip and hand joints [6,7]. The diagnosis of OA is reached by evaluating symp-
toms and plain radiographs, however this approach is not error-free [8]. Knee OA (KOA) is the world’s fourth most prevalent
cause of impairment in both the young and the elderly [9]. According to National Health Interview Survey (NHIS), up to 14
million people in the USA may have knee problems; tens of millions more people may be affected in the Middle East, Europe,
South America or Asia [10]. KOA is a major cause of pain, disability and reduced quality of life globally. Although its preva-
lence is well recognized, the clinical challenge lies in timely and accurate evaluation of the severity of the disease [11–13].
The first line imaging modality of radiographic KOA evaluation remains, but their interpretation is often subjective, and is
accompanied by differences between physicians and the intra-observer variability [14]. Early combined space is ignored
by narrow or osteophyte formations, which lead to delayed diagnosis and inappropriate treatment plans [15]. For orthopedic
surgeons, radiologists and physical therapists, accurate grading of KOA severity is necessary in directing patient manage-
ment [16]. In the initial stages, patients benefit from conservative interventions such as exercise, physiotherapy, weight loss
or medicinal remedies [17]. In contrast, patients with advanced KOA require referrals for surgical consultation, including
joint replacement [18]. Therefore, KOA has direct implications for the patient’s care, treatment selection and result optimiza-
tion, and a reliable and reproducible method to assess severity is required [19–24].
    Artificial intelligence (AI) −based approaches have attracted increasing attention in musculoskeletal imaging, especially
for their ability to provide objective and coherent analysis of radiographs. However, many existing models suffer from two
main boundaries: lack of interpretation, which prevents physicians from understanding a certain classification, and variable
performance in the dataset, which reduces clinical trust. These obstacles have slowed the adoption of AI tools in regular prac-
tice. The proposed Optimized Granger Causality Inspired Graph Neural Network with Accelerated Model-agnostic Explana-
tions–Secretary Bird Optimization Algorithm (KOAC-GCIGNN-AcME-SBOA) offers deep learning-dependent KOA
classification with higher accuracy and computation time function to accomplish the aforementioned goals.
    A novel [25] deep learning framework, KOAC-GCIGNN-AcME-SBOA is proposed. The method integrates advanced feature
extraction, optimized deep learning and explains AI techniques. Beyond obtaining high classification accuracy, the system is
designed to provide transparent outputs that help physicians understand the logic behind automatic grading. By reducing
computational precision with clinical interpretation, this approach has the ability to reduce clinical uncertainty, support
evidence-based treatment decisions and improve patient results in the management of KOA.
    The key contributions of this paper are: (1) The proposed [26] KOAC-GCIGNN-AcME-SBOA system contributes to field-
based knee OA classification utilizing Optimized GCINN with Accelerated Model-agnostic Explanations (AcME). (2) The
images were collected from the Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI) database and preprocessed using the Multi-Window Sav-
itzky–Golay Filter (MWSGF) method for removing artifacts, scaling, managing contrast, and normalizing the picture. (3)
The preprocessed image was fed into Feature Affine Residual Network (FA-ResNet) for feature extraction from the image
and GCINN-AcME using KOA detection. (4) Optimization was achieved by integrating the GCINN with AcME to accurately
represent temporal dependencies with SBOA. (5) The performance of the KOAC-GCIGNN-AcME-SBOA for KOA detection
was compared with other methods to demonstrate the method’s robustness for better performance.

2. Literature review

    There are many research works in the literature on deep learning methods depending on KOA classification; a few recent
works are revised here.
    Abd El-Ghany et al. [27] suggested an automated, precisely calibrated deep learning technique for KOA identification and
advancement evaluation. From the patient’s point of view, the procedure was improved by lowering diagnostic costs, expe-
diting diagnosis, and delaying the progression of disease. Because of this localization, the recommended approach would
help clinicians comprehend the underlying causes of KOA. It offered low precision and high accuracy.
    Yong et al. [28] presented an ordinal regression module for KOA severity classification. Kellgren–Lawrence (KL) grading
was handled as an ordinal regression task using an ordinal regression element for neural networks. Using a neural network
as input, the module generates four cut-points that divide the five KL grades within the prediction space. To optimize, a
cumulative-link loss function was used in the model. The model’s effectiveness was assessed against a number of prominent
neural networks, and the KOA KL grade prediction revealed remarkable improvements. It provided higher recall and lower
specificity.
    Tariq et al. [29] introduced a classification method utilizing X-rays (KOD-CNN-XR). It automatically identifies and cate-
gorizes KOA utilizing radiographs and the KL grading scheme. Through single standing knee X-ray was poster anterior an
automated deep-learning-based ordinal classification strategy for KOA early detection and grading. It provided low precision
and high f1-score.

    Jain et al. [30] presented KOA severity prediction by attentive multi-scale deep convolutional neural network (CNN).
OsteoHRNet uses X-rays to autonomously grade KOA severity according to KL standards. The main innovation was that it
uses the newest deep model using High-Resolution Network (HRNet) to record multiple scale characteristics of X-rays of
the knee. It provided high receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and low computation time.
    Kalpana and Kumar [31] evaluated the efficacy of a deep learning model for KOA prediction based on KL grading system.
Using KOA as a clinical scenario, twelve transfers learning deep learning methods were tested for their accuracy in recogniz-
ing the radiograph’s KOA grade. The optimal model for KOA detection was identified. It was found that the maximum degree
of accuracy was attributable to MobileNet. Here, skilled radiologists and orthopedic specialists have developed deep learning
techniques that could aid smaller hospitals in learning and increasing emergency room visits. It provided higher precision,
but lower f1-score.
    Patil and Salunkhee [32] suggested Classification and Risk Estimation of OA using deep learning methods. The model aims
to better understand its behavior in recognizing extremely complex cases of knee OA. It transitions from a multi-class to a
binary class using a divide-and-conquer method, which enhances the findings’ interpretability and explainability when
explainable AI (XAI) was applied. It had high recall and low specificity.
    Rehman et al. [33] presented a method of KOA diagnosis using transfer learning-basis smart features engineering to
develop a model capable of early and exactly diagnosing KOA X-ray images. A number of machine-learning-based methods
and the sophisticated deep-learning-based CNN method are used in comparison. A new transfer-learning-based feature engi-
neering method called CRK is suggested as a highly effective way to identify OA. It attained low precision and a high f1-score.

3.1. Data acquisition

   Knee X-ray images were derived from the publicly available OAI dataset [34], which contains de-identified images with
KL grading. The baseline cohort of the OAI database consists of 4746 participants’ MRI and X-ray images. In total, 4446 X-ray
images from the complete cohort were selected depending on the evaluations performed by Boston University X-ray reading
center (BU-RC), from which 8892 total knee images were chosen based on the availability of KL ratings for both knees. Here,
the KL grades provided by the BU-RC within the OAI as the gold standard. These KL grades were assigned by experienced
musculoskeletal radiologists following standardized protocols and they served as the reference labels for model training
and evaluation. This dataset has knee X-ray data for knee joint findings together with knee KL grading. This analysis is con-
ducted on the basis of the OAI, a large, multicentric, longitudinal cohort study developed for the purpose of examining the
occurrence of KOA. The following are the demographic variables for which data is available in the database: age, gender, eth-
nicity, height, weight, and body mass index (BMI). The study’s initial dataset includes people across a wide age spectrum and
also includes members from both male and female groups. The study’s dataset includes people with varying levels of BMI,
ranging from normal to obese, and this is important because there is a clear link between obesity and the progression of KOA.

3.5. Optimization utilizing SBOA

   The weights value W j 1 and Y d of the GCIGNN-AcME is improved by SBOA [38].The SBOA is employed to increase the
accuracy and reduce errors, which fixes the parameters of the model. This ensures more reliable classification results. In
addition to semi-desert environments and wooded areas with broad clearings, secretary birds are commonly found in trop-
ical open grasslands, savannahs with few trees, and open areas with long grass. Secretary birds have gray–brown feathers on
their wings and backs, with pristine white on their chests and deep black on their bellies. For the best performance, the
parameters in the GCIGNN-AcME classifier are optimized through the SBOA. In deep learning, parameters in model training
are critical in determining the level of classification, generality, or computational complexity. SBOA is a population-based
optimization technique that is motivated by the strategy that secretary birds use to hunt. It searches for parameter sets that
optimize both classification accuracy and computational complexity by maximizing accuracy and minimizing complexity
simultaneously. Adaptive searching in this process also ensures that a local optimum is not obtained.

4.5. Discussion

    In this work, the KOAC-GCIGNN-AcME-SBOA model for KOA classification is discussed. It enhances KOA classification by
first pre-processing input images key features that make statistics are taken out. The classification model, designed to cap-
ture complex data dependencies, initially lacks parameter optimization. An OA method is used to fine-tune these parame-
ters, improving the model’s accuracy and reliability. This integrated approach of advanced pre-processing, robust feature
extraction and effective optimization leads to superior performance in classifying KOA severity, making it valuable for accu-
rate diagnosis and treatment planning. The proposed method has potential clinical relevance as it enables more reliable
grading of severity of KOA. Accurate discrimination between low and high severity supports orthopedic experts in choosing
appropriate interventions from physiotherapy and lifestyle modifications in early stages to surgical evaluation in advanced
cases. By reducing clinical uncertainty, the proposed model helps to tailor treatment plans, helps to avoid unnecessary pro-
cedures and helps patients to improve results. The experimental assessment of the proposed method is underscored through
a range of mentioned metrics. It concludes that the proposed method is superior to the existing models for KOA
classification.
    From a medical perspective, the proposed structure provides more than better classification accuracy. By distinguishing
between healthy, early stages and advanced KOA, the system supports physicians in treatment strategies. Initial detection of
suspected or minimal KOA grade enables physicians to recommend physical intervention such as physiotherapy, weight
management or lifestyle modifications, potentially to slow the progression of the disease. In moderate to serious cases
the system can help orthopedic surgeons to identify patients requiring surgical consultation, including joint replacement.
In addition, integration of the model’s explanatory output (through AcME) increases transparency, allowing doctors to
understand how a specific grade was assigned. This improves clinical confidence in AI- assisted grading and reduces
inter-perfection variability that usually affects radiographic KOA evaluation. Overall, the method acts as a decision-
supporting tool, which helps radiologists and orthopedic experts to provide timely, personal and evidence-based care.
